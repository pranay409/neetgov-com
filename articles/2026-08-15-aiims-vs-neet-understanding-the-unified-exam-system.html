AIIMS vs NEET: Understanding the Unified Exam System

Published on 2026-08-15 | NEETGov Team

If you're preparing for medical college admission in India, you've probably heard people talking about AIIMS, NEET, and how they changed the entire game. A lot of students get confused about whether these are different exams or the same thing. Let me clear this up for you right away: as of 2023, AIIMS has merged with NEET. There's now a single unified entrance exam for medical admissions across India. This is actually great news for you because it simplifies your preparation strategy.

What Changed and When

Until 2022, AIIMS and NEET were separate exams. AIIMS conducted its own entrance test for admission to AIIMS institutes across India, while NEET was the national level exam for medical and dental colleges. Many students had to prepare for both exams separately, which meant double the work and double the stress. From 2023 onwards, the National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences (NBEMS) decided to integrate AIIMS with NEET. Now there's one exam, one syllabus, and one merit list (though AIIMS seats and NEET seats are still allocated separately based on your rank).

This change affects you directly. You no longer need to solve two different question papers or worry about different exam patterns. Your single NEET score gets you access to government medical seats across AIIMS institutes, JIPMER, and other central and state government medical colleges.

Understanding the Current System

The unified NEET exam now includes all the topics that were previously covered in both AIIMS and NEET papers. The exam is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) and remains the same that you're probably studying for right now. What makes it "unified" is that your score is valid for all government medical college admissions.

Here's what you need to know about seat allocation. AIIMS institutes still have their own quota of seats, and NEET seats are distributed among other government colleges. Your NEET rank determines which college you can get admission into. A rank in the top 100 or 200 might get you an AIIMS seat, while ranks in the top 10,000 can fetch you admission in various state and central government medical colleges.

How This Benefits Your Preparation

The unified system means your preparation now has a single clear target. You don't need to switch between different question patterns or worry about exam strategy variations. Everything you study contributes to one exam and one merit list. This gives you the mental clarity to focus deeply on concepts rather than jumping between multiple preparation plans.

The question difficulty of NEET is comparable to what AIIMS used to ask. If you're preparing with AIIMS level difficulty in mind, you're on the right track. The emphasis on conceptual clarity, quick problem solving, and time management remains the same. Your preparation strategy doesn't need to change because of this merger.

What This Means for Government Medical Seats

Here's the reality: getting into a government medical college is completely achievable. The number of government seats available is substantial, and with the unified system, your chances are clearer than ever. There are several thousand government medical seats across India, and your NEET rank determines your access to these seats. The top performers get AIIMS and premium government colleges, mid-range performers get good state government colleges, and students with decent ranks still secure government medical seats in various institutions.

The key is preparing smartly. You need to understand the important topics, solve quality mock tests regularly, and work on your speed and accuracy. Many students crack into top government colleges every year, and there's absolutely no reason why you can't be one of them.
