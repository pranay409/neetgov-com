NEET Reservation Policy Explained: OBC, SC, ST, EWS Seats

Published on 2026-08-18 | NEETGov Team

Understanding the NEET reservation policy is absolutely crucial if you're aiming for a government medical college seat. Many students don't realize that their category eligibility could significantly impact their cutoff score and selection chances. The good news? With the right knowledge and preparation strategy, you can navigate this system effectively and secure an excellent medical college seat.

What is NEET Reservation and Why Does It Matter?

The Indian government reserves a specific percentage of medical college seats for students belonging to SC (Scheduled Caste), ST (Scheduled Tribe), OBC (Other Backward Class), and EWS (Economically Weaker Section) categories. This policy aims to provide equal opportunities for underrepresented groups in higher education. For you as a NEET aspirant, this means the cutoff scores vary significantly by category, which directly affects your chances of getting admission.

Here's the thing most students miss: even if you don't fall into a reserved category, understanding how the policy works helps you benchmark your preparation properly. And if you do belong to a reserved category, knowing your actual cutoff (which is typically much lower) can boost your confidence and help you set realistic targets.

Breaking Down the Reservation Categories

Scheduled Caste (SC) Seats

SC students get 15% of all government medical college seats across India. If you belong to this category, your cutoff score will be considerably lower than the general category cutoff. For example, if the general cutoff for a state medical college is around 600 marks, the SC cutoff might range between 350 to 450 marks depending on the college tier and state. You'll need a valid SC certificate from your state authorities to claim this reservation.

Scheduled Tribe (ST) Seats

ST category students receive 7.5% of government medical college seats. The cutoff for ST candidates is typically the lowest among reserved categories because the ST population is smaller. Your cutoff score could be anywhere from 300 to 400 marks depending on the specific college and state. This is a significant advantage if you belong to this category. Always ensure your ST certificate is valid and issued by the correct authority.

Other Backward Class (OBC) Seats

OBC students get 27% of government medical college seats, making this the largest reserved category. The cutoff for OBC is higher than SC and ST but still considerably lower than general category cutoffs. You might see OBC cutoffs ranging from 450 to 550 marks for decent colleges. Keep in mind that OBC status is determined by a centralized list maintained by the government, and your state OBC certificate should match this list.

Economically Weaker Section (EWS) Seats

EWS is the newest reservation category introduced in 2019, allocating 10% of seats. This applies to students from the general category whose family income is below a specified threshold (currently around Rs 8 lakh per annum). The EWS cutoff is higher than SC, ST, and OBC but lower than the unreserved general category. You'll need an EWS certificate along with income proof to claim this reservation.

How This Impacts Your Medical College Admission

The practical impact is significant. Suppose the general category cutoff is 650 marks for a state medical college. You might see the OBC cutoff at 520 marks, SC at 380 marks, and ST at 320 marks. This massive difference means your category directly determines which colleges are realistically within your reach based on your NEET score.

Many students prepare without clearly understanding their target cutoff. If you're in the SC or ST category and have prepared to score 400 marks, that could genuinely get you into a good government medical college. But if you're preparing as if you're unreserved and targeting 650 marks, you might unnecessarily stress yourself out.

Important Points About Reservation Policy

  • You can only claim one reserved category. If you have both SC and OBC eligibility, you must choose one for that particular exam.
  • Caste certificates must be issued by the appropriate government authority in your state. Fake certificates are taken very seriously and can lead to legal action.
  • EWS status needs to be verified through income certificates that match the current annual income threshold.
  • All government medical colleges follow the same reservation percentages set by the central government.
  • Private medical colleges are generally not bound by these reservation policies.
  • The reservation policy applies at both the state counseling level and the NEET All India Quota level.

Planning Your Preparation Strategy

Here's what you should do right now: identify your exact category and find out the average cutoff scores for your category in the states where you want admission. This gives you a realistic target. If your category has a lower cutoff, that's excellent news for your admission prospects. Use this to set achievable scoring targets for your preparation.

Don't let this become an excuse to prepare less rigorously though. Higher scores within your category category mean access to better colleges and better branches. So whether you're aiming for 400 or 600 marks, prepare thoroughly with structured coaching that understands the NEET pattern deeply.
