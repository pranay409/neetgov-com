NEET Reservation Policy Explained: OBC, SC, ST, EWS Seats

If you're preparing for NEET and aiming for a government medical college, understanding the reservation policy is absolutely crucial. Many students focus only on scoring high marks but miss out on how reservations can actually work in their favor. The truth is, knowing your eligibility and how seats are allocated can be the difference between getting into a government college or not. Let me break down the entire reservation system in a way that actually makes sense.

What is the NEET Reservation System?

The Indian government reserves a certain percentage of medical college seats for candidates belonging to specific categories. This system exists to ensure equal opportunities for historically disadvantaged communities. Out of the total seats available in government medical colleges across India, a fixed percentage is allocated to OBC, SC, ST candidates, and a newer category called EWS. The remaining seats are open for everyone, but in practice, candidates from unreserved categories compete for these open seats.

The exact percentages are set at the national level and are binding on all government medical colleges. These percentages remain consistent whether you're applying to AIIMS, government medical colleges, or deemed universities that follow the NEET counselling process.

SC Reservation: 15% of Seats

Scheduled Caste candidates get 15% of the total seats reserved in government medical colleges. To avail this reservation, you need to possess a valid SC certificate issued by the competent authority of your state or union territory. The certificate must be issued before the deadline specified by the counselling authority.

An important point: SC reservation is across India, meaning an SC candidate from any state can apply for SC reserved seats in any state's medical colleges. The cutoff for SC reserved seats is typically lower than the general category, which gives you a better chance of getting admission with a relatively lower rank.

ST Reservation: 7.5% of Seats

Scheduled Tribe candidates receive 7.5% reservation. Like SC candidates, you'll need a valid ST certificate. What many students don't realize is that ST reserved seats sometimes have even lower cutoffs compared to SC reserved seats because fewer candidates typically fall into this category in many regions.

If you're an ST candidate, your chances of getting into a good government medical college are actually quite strong, even with a moderate rank. The strategy should be to aim for your preferred colleges under ST category rather than competing in the open category.

OBC Reservation: 27% of Seats

Other Backward Classes get 27% reservation, which is the largest reservation chunk. This is further divided into Non-Creamy Layer (NCL) and Creamy Layer. You need an OBC certificate, and importantly, your family income should be below the specified threshold to qualify as OBC NCL. If your family income exceeds the limit, you'll be categorized as OBC Creamy Layer and won't get the reservation benefit.

The OBC NCL category is quite popular, so the cutoff is higher than SC/ST but still significantly lower than the general category. Many OBC candidates successfully secure seats in their state medical colleges or even all-India quota seats with moderate ranks.

EWS Reservation: 10% of Seats

This is the newest category, introduced in 2019. EWS stands for Economically Weaker Section and is open to candidates from the general category (not SC, ST, or OBC) whose family income is below Rs 8 lakh per annum. This is a significant opportunity for economically backward candidates who don't have any other reservation.

The beauty of EWS is that it's a relatively new category with gradually increasing awareness. The cutoff for EWS seats is typically between the general and OBC categories, making it an accessible pathway for eligible candidates.

The Open Category: 40.5% of Seats

The remaining seats are open for all candidates, regardless of category. However, candidates with SC/ST/OBC/EWS certificates can also compete in the open category if they choose. The open category has the highest cutoff since it's the most competitive pool. Many general category candidates secure medical seats here, but you need a strong rank.

Practical Points You Must Know

  • Your certificate category is fixed and based on your permanent residence state. You cannot change it for counselling.
  • If you're eligible for multiple categories (for example, both OBC and EWS), you typically choose one during counselling based on which gives you the best college option.
  • All-India quota seats (15% of government medical college seats) also follow the same reservation percentages.
  • State quota seats follow the same national-level percentages for SC/ST/OBC/EWS, but many states have additional state-specific reservations for their domicile candidates.
  • Getting a reservation doesn't lower the difficulty of the NEET exam. You still need to study hard and score well. The reservation only helps during the counselling process when your rank is compared with others in your category.

Strategy for NEET Preparation

Know your category and understand what rank you realistically need to secure a government college seat. This knowledge should motivate you, not demotivate you. If you're an SC/ST/OBC/EWS candidate, you have a mathematical advantage during counselling. Use this to set realistic goals and work towards them consistently.

The key is having structured preparation with the right guidance. Many students waste months with inefficient study methods when they could be making real progress with proper coaching and mentoring.
