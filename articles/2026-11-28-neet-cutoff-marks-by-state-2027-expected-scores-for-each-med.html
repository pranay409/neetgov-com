NEET Cutoff Marks by State 2027: Expected Scores for Each Medical College

Planning your NEET preparation for 2027? One of the biggest questions every student asks is: what cutoff score do I need to get into a government medical college in my state? This is actually the right question to ask, because understanding state-wise cutoffs helps you set realistic targets and prepare accordingly. Let me break down what you need to know about NEET cutoffs for 2027 and how to approach this strategically.

Why Cutoff Scores Vary So Much by State

First, understand that NEET cutoffs are not fixed numbers that NTA announces like a universal rule. Instead, cutoffs are determined by state medical authorities and colleges based on several factors: the number of seats available, the difficulty of that year's exam, the number of qualified candidates, and reservation policies in that state. This means every state has different cutoff ranges, and even different colleges within the same state can have different scores.

When you appear for NEET 2027, you'll get a percentile rank, and based on that, different states will call you for counselling if you meet their minimum cutoff. This is why students often ask: "What score should I target?" The honest answer is that it depends on which state you're applying to and which college tier you're aiming for.

Expected Cutoff Ranges for 2027

Based on trends from previous years, here's what you should expect. Keep in mind these are approximate ranges because actual cutoffs depend on that year's difficulty level and the number of candidates:

  • Top tier government colleges (like AIIMS, top Delhi colleges, etc.): 650 to 720 marks. These are highly competitive and attract the best candidates nationwide. If you're targeting these, you need to be consistently scoring in this range during your practice tests.
  • Premium state medical colleges (state capitals, well-known institutions): 580 to 650 marks. These colleges have good placements and infrastructure, making them popular choices.
  • Tier 2 state medical colleges: 500 to 580 marks. These are good colleges with decent opportunities, available in most states.
  • Tier 3 and newly established colleges: 400 to 500 marks. These colleges are more accessible but may have fewer job opportunities.

State-Specific Variations You Should Know

Different states have different patterns. For example, states with more medical colleges typically have lower cutoffs because there are more seats to fill. States like Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Karnataka have multiple colleges, so cutoffs tend to be slightly lower than metro-centric states. On the other hand, states like Delhi and Haryana have fewer seats and higher competition, leading to higher cutoffs.

If you're from a reserved category (OBC, SC, ST), your cutoff will be lower than the general category cutoff by approximately 10-15% depending on your state's rules. This is important information because it changes your target score significantly.

How to Use This Information in Your Preparation

Here's what you should do right now. First, identify which state you want to study in. Then, look at which colleges in that state interest you. Check the previous year's cutoffs for those specific colleges. For 2027, expect cutoffs to be similar to 2026 or 2025 unless the exam difficulty changes drastically. Set your target score slightly higher than the previous year's cutoff, maybe add 20-30 marks as a safety buffer.

For instance, if a college's 2026 cutoff was 580, aim for 600-610 in your practice tests to have a comfortable margin. This approach keeps you motivated and gives you realistic targets to work towards.

Important Things to Remember

Cutoff scores are not just about the marks you get. They're about your all India rank or state rank, which depends on how many students score higher than you. A score of 600 might get you a good rank in one year but a different rank in another year. This is why benchmarking against previous years is useful but not foolproof.

Also, remember that NTA releases official cutoffs only after the exam. During your preparation, you should use these historical cutoffs as guidelines, not gospel truth. Your focus should be on maximizing your own performance, not obsessing over exact cutoff numbers.
