NEET PG vs NEET UG: Key Differences for Aspiring Doctors

Published on 2026-08-23 | NEETGov Team

If you're scrolling through NEETGov right now, you're probably wondering about your path into government medical colleges. One question keeps coming up: should I focus on NEET UG or NEET PG? The honest answer is that both matter, but they're completely different beasts. Let me break down exactly what separates them so you can plan your medical career with confidence.

What Are NEET UG and NEET PG?

NEET UG (Undergraduate) is the exam you take right after your 12th standard board exams. This is your entry ticket into government medical colleges for your MBBS degree. You've probably heard about this already because it's the most talked about medical entrance exam in India.

NEET PG (Postgraduate) comes later in your career. After you finish your MBBS and internship, if you want to specialize in fields like surgery, pediatrics, or psychiatry, you need to crack NEET PG. This exam determines which government medical college and which specialty you'll pursue for your MD or MS degree.

Educational Qualification: Where You Stand Matters

To appear for NEET UG, you need to have completed your 12th with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. That's it. Most students start preparing for NEET UG in their 11th itself, giving them roughly 18 to 24 months of preparation time.

NEET PG has a different requirement entirely. You must have completed your MBBS degree and finished your internship. This typically means you're appearing for NEET PG around 4 to 5 years after your NEET UG. So the timeline is completely different here.

The Syllabus: What You'll Actually Study

NEET UG covers the school-level science curriculum: 11th and 12th Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. The questions test your conceptual understanding of basics like organs, cells, chemical reactions, and mechanics. Most topics you study for NEET UG are directly from your NCERT textbooks.

NEET PG is a whole different animal. You're tested on clinical knowledge accumulated during your MBBS and internship. The questions are scenario-based and require you to think like a practicing doctor. You'll answer questions about patient management, disease diagnosis, treatment protocols, and clinical decision-making. Nothing in NEET PG comes from school textbooks.

Difficulty Level and Competition

NEET UG has massive competition. Over 18 to 20 lakh students typically appear for NEET UG every year. But here's the good news: many of these students don't prepare seriously or run out of time. If you follow a structured plan starting from 11th standard, you're already ahead of the curve. Government medical seats are absolutely achievable with focused preparation.

NEET PG has fewer candidates appearing, usually around 2 to 3 lakh students annually. However, these are all doctors who've already completed their MBBS. Competition is comparatively fierce because nearly everyone attempting it has formal medical training.

Preparation Duration and Strategy

Most successful NEET UG candidates prepare for 18 to 24 months. You can start your preparation in 11th standard and continue through 12th. This longer preparation window allows you to build concepts gradually, revise multiple times, and take plenty of full-length practice tests.

NEET PG preparation typically runs for 6 to 12 months. You're juggling your medical college studies and internship while preparing, so it's more intense even though the duration is shorter. Many NEET PG aspirants study while working or completing their final clinical rotations.

Selection and Seat Allocation

After clearing NEET UG, you participate in counseling where government and private medical colleges offer seats based on your rank and category. You get an MBBS seat at a government medical college, which is the ultimate goal for most students right now.

After clearing NEET PG, you enter a different counseling process where you choose your specialty and college. You might get admitted to your preferred institute for super specialization, or you get allocated based on your rank and choices. The stakes are high because you're selecting your professional specialty here.

Cost and Financial Planning

NEET UG coaching typically costs between 20000 to 50000 rupees for a year-long program. Most students invest in coaching for the full 24-month period. Government medical college MBBS is essentially free or very low cost once you're admitted.

NEET PG coaching is more expensive, ranging from 40000 to 100000 rupees because it's specialized clinical content. However, by the time you're doing NEET PG, you're likely to have more financial resources as you're completing your MBBS.

Which One Should You Focus On Right Now?

If you're currently in 10th or 11th standard, forget about NEET PG for now. Concentrate entirely on NEET UG. Your MBBS degree is the foundation for everything else in your medical career. You cannot skip NEET UG and go straight to NEET PG.

Many students get discouraged thinking NEET UG is too difficult. Let me tell you clearly: with proper coaching, consistent study habits, and smart time management, government medical seats are absolutely within reach. Thousands of students crack it every year, and so can you.
