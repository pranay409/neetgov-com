NEET Eligibility Criteria 2027: Age Limit, Attempts, and Qualifications

Getting into a government medical college is absolutely within your reach, but first things first – you need to make sure you meet the NEET eligibility criteria. Every year, thousands of students get confused about the basic requirements, and some even disqualify themselves unknowingly. So let's clear this up once and for all. Understanding the eligibility criteria is your first step toward a successful NEET journey, and honestly, it's simpler than you might think.

Age Limit Requirements for NEET 2027

One of the most common questions we get is about age limits. Here's the good news: there's no upper age limit for NEET. Seriously, if you're 25, 30, or even older, you can still appear for NEET and qualify. However, there is a minimum age requirement that you need to be aware of.

To be eligible for NEET 2027, you must have completed 17 years of age on or before October 31st of the year of admission. That's the official cutoff. So if you're appearing in NEET 2027 for admission to medical college starting 2027-28, you should be at least 17 years old by October 31, 2027. Most of you reading this will easily meet this requirement, but it's worth double-checking if you're on the younger side.

Some states also have their own age criteria for state counseling, so once you clear NEET, check with your state's medical education board about any additional age requirements. But nationally, 17 years is your magic number.

Educational Qualifications You Must Have

Let's talk about what subjects and qualifications you need. To appear for NEET, you must have completed your 12th standard (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology as compulsory subjects. This is non-negotiable. Your board doesn't really matter – CBSE, state boards, international boards – as long as you have these three subjects, you're good.

You should have scored at least 50% marks in Physics, Chemistry, and Biology combined (this goes down to 40% if you belong to SC/ST/OBC categories). This is just for eligibility though. Your actual NEET score is what will determine which college you get into, so don't just aim for 50%, aim much higher.

If you're in your 12th right now, focus on completing your board exams first. You can apply for NEET as soon as your 12th results are declared. If your state's board is slow in declaring results, don't panic – the NEET application deadline is usually set considering this.

Number of Attempts You Can Take

Here's something that takes pressure off: there's no limit on the number of attempts you can take for NEET. You can appear as many times as you want. However, the thing to remember is that only your best score is considered for admission. If you appear multiple times, your highest score is what matters.

That said, appearing for NEET multiple times can be mentally draining and financially taxing (registration fees add up). The smarter approach is to prepare properly the first time itself. With focused preparation and the right coaching, most students crack NEET in their first or second attempt.

If you don't get the desired rank in your first attempt, you can definitely retake it. Many students improve significantly in their second attempt because they know exactly what to expect and can fix their weak areas.

Reservation Categories and Special Eligibility

If you belong to SC, ST, OBC, or EWS categories, there are specific benefits in terms of cutoff marks and seat reservations. Your category will be based on your state government's official OBC list. Make sure your category is properly registered during the application process.

There are also separate eligibility criteria and quotas for persons with disabilities (PwD). If you fall under this category, check the official NTA website for specific requirements.

Practical Tips for Checking Your Eligibility

  • Verify your 12th marks in Physics, Chemistry, and Biology meet the 50% threshold (or 40% for reserved categories)
  • Calculate your age and confirm you'll be at least 17 by October 31 of the admission year
  • Get your 12th certificate or admit card ready for the application process
  • Check if you have any state-specific requirements through your state medical education board's website
  • Keep your category documentation ready if you belong to SC/ST/OBC/EWS

Start Your Preparation Now

Once you've confirmed you meet all eligibility criteria, the real work begins. NEET requires serious, structured preparation. You need to cover an enormous syllabus accurately, practice thousands of questions, and develop the right test-taking strategy. This isn't something you can do haphazardly.
