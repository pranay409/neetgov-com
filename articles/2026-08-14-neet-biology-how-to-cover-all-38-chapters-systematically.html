NEET Biology: How to Cover All 38 Chapters Systematically

Published on 2026-08-14 | NEETGov Team

Biology is often called the "scoring" section in NEET, but only if you approach it strategically. With 38 chapters spread across Class 11 and 12, the challenge isn't really the difficulty—it's the vastness of the syllabus. Many students get overwhelmed and end up studying randomly, missing crucial topics or wasting time on less important chapters. If you're serious about securing a government medical seat, you need a clear roadmap for covering all 38 chapters systematically.

Let me walk you through how to organize your biology preparation so that every chapter gets proper attention and nothing slips through the cracks.

Understanding the Weight of Each Chapter

Not all chapters are equally important in NEET. Some chapters have appeared repeatedly with 4-5 questions each, while others typically get 1-2 questions. This doesn't mean you ignore the smaller chapters, but it means you allocate your time differently.

The heavy hitters in NEET biology are chapters like Genetics, Evolution, Reproduction, Cell Biology, Photosynthesis, and Ecology. These typically account for 25-30% of the entire biology section. Then you have medium-weight chapters like Digestion, Respiration, and Nervous System that contribute another 20-25%. The remaining chapters, while important, are relatively lighter in terms of question frequency.

Start by making a list of all 38 chapters with a difficulty rating from 1 to 5. This helps you decide whether to spend 3 hours on a chapter or 1.5 hours.

Breaking Down the 38 Chapters by Time Allocation

Here's a realistic breakdown for covering all chapters:

Class 11 Biology (19 Chapters)

  • Cell Biology and Cell Division: 8-10 hours (crucial foundation)
  • Biomolecules and Enzymes: 6-7 hours (concepts appear everywhere)
  • Photosynthesis: 5-6 hours (questions are concept-heavy)
  • Plant Anatomy: 3-4 hours (straightforward, fewer complications)
  • Animal Tissues: 2-3 hours (mostly memorization with some conceptual parts)
  • Digestion and Absorption: 4-5 hours (good number of questions)
  • Respiration: 5-6 hours (calculations and concepts mixed)
  • Body Fluids and Circulation: 4-5 hours (important for understanding physiology)
  • Excretion: 3-4 hours (relatively straightforward)
  • Locomotion and Movement: 2-3 hours (mostly diagrams and structure)
  • Neural Control and Coordination: 5-6 hours (lots of pathway questions)
  • Chemical Coordination: 4-5 hours (hormones and their effects)
  • Reproduction in Plants: 3-4 hours (good conceptual chapter)
  • Reproduction in Humans: 4-5 hours (frequent questions)
  • Gametogenesis: 3-4 hours (part of reproduction unit)
  • Fertilization and Development: 4-5 hours (important for understanding embryology)
  • Immune System: 3-4 hours (practical relevance, moderate difficulty)
  • Microbes and Disease: 2-3 hours (interesting but lighter in questions)
  • Plant Diseases: 1-2 hours (rarely tested deeply)

Class 12 Biology (19 Chapters)

  • Genetics and Heredity: 8-10 hours (one of the highest priority chapters)
  • Molecular Basis of Inheritance: 6-7 hours (DNA, RNA, protein synthesis)
  • Evolution: 5-6 hours (theory and evidence combined)
  • Human Health and Disease: 4-5 hours (relevant and interesting)
  • Strategies for Enhancement of Food Production: 3-4 hours (agriculture-focused)
  • Microbes in Human Welfare: 2-3 hours (applications-based)
  • Biotechnology: 4-5 hours (modern, frequently asked)
  • Organisms and Populations: 3-4 hours (ecology basics)
  • Ecosystem: 4-5 hours (energy flow, cycles, important concepts)
  • Biodiversity and Conservation: 3-4 hours (current relevance)
  • Environmental Issues: 2-3 hours (sometimes asked, good knowledge)
  • Plant Physiology: 5-6 hours (photosynthesis revisited, plant hormones)
  • Mineral Nutrition: 3-4 hours (important for understanding plant growth)
  • Photosynthesis (Advanced): 4-5 hours (if covered separately in your curriculum)
  • Respiration (Advanced): 3-4 hours (pathways and regulation)
  • Nitrogen Cycle: 2-3 hours (part of ecology and biogeochemical cycles)
  • Water Relations: 2-3 hours (osmosis and plant water relations)
  • Transport in Plants: 3-4 hours (phloem and xylem)
  • Human Reproduction (Detailed): 3-4 hours (hormonal control and gametogenesis details)
