NEET Eligibility Criteria 2027: Age Limit, Attempts, and Qualifications

So you're thinking about giving NEET in 2027? That's great! But before you dive into preparation, let's make sure you actually meet the eligibility criteria. I know it sounds boring, but trust me, checking this first will save you months of wasted effort. I've seen students prepare for over a year only to realize they didn't meet some requirement. Let's not let that happen to you.

Age Limit: What You Need to Know

The National Board of Examination (NBE) has set clear age requirements for NEET 2027. You need to be at least 17 years old on or before December 31, 2027, to be eligible. This means if you're currently in 12th grade and planning to appear for NEET in 2027, you should check your birth date carefully.

Here's something most students miss: there's no upper age limit for NEET! This is actually great news because it means you can appear for the exam even if you're taking a gap year or coming back after a break. I've personally seen students who took 2-3 years after 12th and still got excellent ranks.

The only age-related rule you need to remember is that you must have completed your 17 years by the exam date. If you turn 17 on January 1, 2028, you won't be eligible for the 2027 exam. So check your exact date of birth against the exam schedule.

Educational Qualifications: What Board You're From Doesn't Matter

You need to have passed your 12th grade (or equivalent) from a recognized board with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology as main subjects. The board doesn't matter. Whether you're from CBSE, ICSE, State Board, or even international boards like IB or Cambridge, you're eligible.

The key requirement is that you must have studied Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and English in 12th. If you've taken any other combination, you won't be eligible. So if you took Math instead of Biology, unfortunately, you can't apply for NEET.

Your 12th marks don't need to be great for NEET eligibility. There's no minimum percentage requirement. I know students with 50% in 12th who've gotten into government colleges. NEET is purely merit-based on the exam score, not on your board marks.

Number of Attempts: How Many Chances You Have

This is where it gets interesting. NEET can be attempted multiple times with no limit on the number of attempts. You can keep taking it until you get the score you want. However, there are practical limits based on the age requirement I mentioned earlier.

Since there's no upper age limit, theoretically you could attempt NEET in 2027, 2028, 2029, and beyond. But practically, most students prepare for 1-2 years before getting their desired score. The good news? You have time to get it right.

One thing to note: NEET is held only once a year now, usually in May. So you can attempt it only once per year. If you miss it in 2027, you'll have to wait for 2028. Plan your preparation timeline accordingly.

Nationality and Domicile Requirements

You must be an Indian citizen, overseas citizen of India (OCI), or person of Indian origin (PIO) to be eligible for NEET. There's no domicile requirement for most colleges, but some state-based colleges have their own state-specific rules. When you start looking at college options, check if your state has any such restrictions.

Special Category Candidates

If you have any disability (physical or otherwise), you can get special consideration. The exam authorities provide extra time, scribes, and other facilities. There's no relaxation in the qualifying marks, but you get practical support during the exam. Make sure to mention this in your application.

What If You Don't Meet Eligibility Yet?

If you haven't passed 12th yet, focus on completing it with Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and English. This is your foundation anyway. If you're worried about your 12th percentage, don't be. It literally doesn't matter for NEET admissions.

Start Your Preparation Now

Once you've confirmed you're eligible, the real work begins. NEET requires dedicated preparation. The syllabus is vast, covering 12th and 11th grade sciences in detail. You need a structured study plan and proper guidance to cover everything and solve practice questions.
