NEET Eligibility Criteria 2027: Age Limit, Attempts, and Qualifications

Published on 2026-08-30 | NEETGov Team

If you're thinking about taking NEET in 2027 to secure a seat in a government medical college, the first thing you need to check is whether you actually meet the eligibility criteria. I know it sounds obvious, but every year some students waste months of preparation only to realize they don't qualify. Let's break down everything you need to know about NEET eligibility for 2027, so you can start your preparation with confidence.

Age Limit Requirements for NEET 2027

The National Medical Commission has set clear age limits for NEET aspirants. For the general category, you need to be at least 17 years old as of December 31st in the year you appear for the exam. There's no upper age limit for attempting NEET, which is great news if you're an older student considering a career change or if you're retaking the exam.

However, there's an important catch here. If you're below 17 years old on December 31st of the year you take NEET, you won't be eligible for admission to MBBS or BDS programs, even if you score well. So if you're 16 right now and planning to appear in 2027, make sure you'll be 17 by December 31st of that year.

For SC, ST, and OBC candidates, there's no relaxation in the age limit itself, though they do get relaxation in the number of attempts available. This is an important distinction to keep in mind while planning your NEET journey.

Educational Qualifications You Need

To be eligible for NEET, you must have passed your 12th board examination with Physics, Chemistry, and Biology as compulsory subjects. Your board doesn't matter—whether you're from CBSE, state boards, ICSE, or any other recognized board—as long as you have these three core subjects. If you took any vocational subject alongside these, that's fine too.

Your 12th percentage doesn't matter for NEET eligibility either. Even if you scored 45 percent in 12th, you can still take NEET. What matters is that you pass the exam. However, for admission to government medical colleges through NEET, most colleges have cutoff scores that require you to be in the top percentile, so your actual NEET score becomes crucial.

If you're an international student or took your 12th from outside India, you'll need to get your qualifications recognized by an appropriate authority. The eligibility rules can get a bit tricky in these cases, so it's worth checking with the official NMC guidelines if this applies to you.

Number of Attempts Allowed

This is where many students get confused. For general category candidates, there's no limit on the number of attempts you can make for NEET. You can attempt it as many times as you want. However, the catch is that you need to meet the basic eligibility criteria each time—having passed 12th with PCB.

For SC, ST, and OBC candidates, the same rule applies—no limit on attempts. However, some state-level NEET counselling processes may have their own restrictions, so check with your specific state's medical education board.

The number of attempts people typically talk about is not an official NEET restriction but rather a practical limit. Most students aim to crack NEET within 2-3 attempts because each attempt takes serious preparation time. If you're planning to appear multiple times, make sure each attempt is backed by genuine improvement in your preparation strategy.

Other Important Eligibility Points

You cannot have appeared for 12th examination more than three years before the NEET exam you're taking. If you passed 12th in 2023, you can still take NEET in 2027 without issues. But if you passed in 2022, you're within the window. Just make sure you're not too far behind.

You also need to be an Indian citizen, an NRI, an OCI cardholder, or a PIO to be eligible for NEET. If you fall into any of these categories, you're good to go. Your residential status doesn't matter as much as your citizenship status.

Getting Your Preparation Right

Knowing the eligibility criteria is just the first step. The real challenge is preparing well enough to score high enough for a government medical seat. Most government colleges have cutoff scores that require you to score in the 600 to 720+ range out of 720, depending on the college and category.
