NEET Chemistry Strategies: Organic vs Inorganic Weightage Analysis

Published on 2026-08-13 | NEETGov Team

Chemistry is the scoring subject in NEET if you approach it the right way. Many students struggle because they don't understand the weightage distribution between organic and inorganic chemistry. Let me break this down for you so you can plan your preparation strategically and maximize your marks.

Understanding the Weightage Distribution

In NEET, chemistry carries 180 marks out of 720, with 45 questions total. The distribution isn't equal between organic and inorganic chemistry, and knowing this helps you allocate your study time wisely.

Typically, you'll see around 25 to 30 percent of chemistry questions coming from organic chemistry and about 35 to 40 percent from inorganic chemistry. The remaining 30 to 35 percent comes from physical chemistry. This means inorganic chemistry actually deserves more of your attention than many students realize.

Inorganic chemistry covers topics like periodic table, coordination compounds, d and f block elements, and qualitative analysis. These are straightforward topics where you either know the facts or you don't. There's less conceptual complexity compared to organic chemistry.

Why Inorganic Chemistry is Your Quick Win

Here's what most toppers know: inorganic chemistry questions test your memory and understanding of properties rather than problem-solving skills. If you've memorized the periodic table trends, you can score 90 percent of inorganic marks easily.

Start by creating tables for each block. Write down electronic configurations, oxidation states, colors, and important reactions. For example, with the p-block alone, you're looking at 8 to 10 questions in NEET. That's roughly 30 to 40 marks if you prepare it right.

Coordination compounds is another goldmine. Learn the basic concepts like CFSE, crystal field splitting, and then focus on naming conventions and properties. This topic gives you 3 to 4 questions consistently.

Qualitative analysis might seem tough, but it's actually pattern-based. Once you understand which ions give which colors and precipitates, you can solve most questions in seconds.

Organic Chemistry: The Strategic Approach

Organic chemistry requires a different mindset. You can't just memorize reactions. You need to understand the mechanism and why each reaction happens. This is where many students waste time without getting results.

Focus on the highest-weightage topics first. Hydrocarbons, alcohols, aldehydes and ketones, carboxylic acids, and amines appear in almost every NEET paper. Master the reaction patterns of these functional groups.

Don't try to learn every reaction for every molecule. Instead, learn the mechanism once and apply it to similar compounds. For instance, if you understand SN1 and SN2 mechanisms, you can solve 80 percent of substitution questions across different organic compounds.

NEET organic chemistry is more about recognizing patterns than memorizing. A question might show you a complex structure and ask which reagent would work. If you understand the underlying principles, you can deduce the answer even if you've never seen that exact reaction before.

Physical Chemistry Sits in Between

Physical chemistry has concepts like thermodynamics, kinetics, and equilibrium. These require both understanding and calculation. Spend about 20 to 25 percent of your chemistry prep time here. Master stoichiometry calculations because they appear across all three sections.

Electrochemistry and redox reactions bridge organic and inorganic sections. Once you're solid on these, many organic oxidation and reduction reactions make sense.

Your Weekly Study Plan

Allocate your chemistry study time like this: 40 percent to inorganic chemistry, 35 percent to organic chemistry, and 25 percent to physical chemistry. This ratio matches the NEET weightage and ensures you're not wasting time on low-weightage topics.

Every week, solve at least 50 chemistry questions mixed from all three sections. This prevents you from getting comfortable with just one area and keeps your overall speed sharp.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't study organic chemistry without writing out the reactions. Physical writing helps your brain retain mechanisms better than just reading or watching videos.

Don't skip inorganic chemistry thinking it's just rote learning. Yes, memory matters, but understanding trends and properties will help you apply knowledge to new situations that NEET throws at you.

Don't solve practice questions without reviewing your mistakes. Every wrong answer is data. Track which topics cause you trouble and revise them more frequently.
